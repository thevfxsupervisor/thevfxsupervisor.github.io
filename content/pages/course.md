## Who it is for

Two people end up in this seat. The independent VFX supervisor or producer who takes on a film with no coordinator, no second supervisor, and a budget and schedule that still have to hold up. And the junior supervisor or producer, employed or newly freelance, handed a whole show for the first time and finding out that "the department" is just them.

Different career stage, same problem: the breakdown and the budget have to be right, and there is no one else to catch what you miss. If that is your seat, this is built from doing that job, not from teaching around it.

## What it is

Not a tutorial on one tool. A method for carrying a whole film's VFX breakdown and budget alone: what to automate, what stays a judgment call, and how to brief an AI assistant to carry the mechanical half without losing control of the numbers. [Breakdown Studio](/projects/breakdown-studio/) is the reference implementation, built and used on real production work including a Swedish Netflix feature. The course is how to run the method on your own show, with or without it.

<!-- included -->
### Reading a cut like a budget
Turning an edit into a shot count and a VFX subset you can defend, before a single bid goes out. Whether it is your fifth film or your first.

### The pipeline that survives a re-cut
Building a breakdown that updates instead of getting rebuilt every time the picture changes, so a late note from the director does not cost you a weekend.

### Judgment versus automation
Where to let a tool run unattended, and where a supervisor's eye has to stay in the loop, so handing off the mechanical work never means losing control of the numbers.

### Briefing an AI pair with guardrails
Getting real leverage from an AI assistant on a live production without handing it the master file, even when you are the only one who would catch it if something went wrong.

## What you leave with

A working method for turning a cut into a shot count and a defensible VFX subset. A breakdown structure that updates through a re-cut instead of being rebuilt. And a clear line, drawn by you, between where a tool can run unattended and where your judgment has to stay in the loop.

You also leave with the working templates: the breakdown sheet, the bid comparison, the change-order log, and the complexity band rubric used during the session.

You do not leave with Breakdown Studio itself. What you build the method in afterwards is your choice.

## Who should not buy this

If you are looking for a tool you install and press go, this is not that. It is a method, and it assumes you are the one making the calls on your show rather than looking to hand the job off.

If you have never broken down a cut or built a bid, this will move fast.

And if you are not currently running, or about to run, a breakdown and budget alone, most of it will not land. Come back when you are.
