Cheaper production tools are arriving, and the money is moving towards whoever holds the rights. A Korean studio uses AI for crowds and keeps cameras for close-ups. Ramayana's reported effects spend shows what big VFX bets cost when the story is wrong. Expect bid conversations to turn on price pressure and ownership.

## Ramayana and the cost of VFX

Update: new detail is the budget logic and the box office. Reported cost for both parts is about ₹4,000 Cr, and the piece says effects pay back only when they serve a story audiences already want. For bids, expect producers to scrutinise effects shares and to push risk onto vendors.

- [VFX Budget in Indian Movies: Who Pays, and When It Pays Back](https://www.sacnilk.com/news/vfx-budget-in-indian-movies-who-pays-and-when-it-pays-back) · sacnilk.com *Effects shares of 25% to 30% on big films and reported losses past ₹100 Cr on spectacle-led ones mean tougher budget questions for vendors*

## Tools and pipeline watch

Two tool stories point at labour-heavy tasks: multi-person motion capture from ordinary cameras, and AI-assisted rotoscoping. Neither is proven in production here, so treat both as things to test, not to quote on. If they hold up, they could change how you price capture and roto hours.

- [MAMMA Mocap: iPhones Replace $10K Rigs](https://shattered.io/mamma-smartphone-motion-capture-max-planck-cmu-iphones-two-labs-zero-prices/) · shattered.io *Phone-based multi-person capture could cut stage costs for small teams, but the coverage gives no accuracy figures, price or release date*
- [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/) · fxguide.com *Foundry's new AI roto plugin for Nuke targets a tedious task, so it may change how you budget roto hours; the Stranger Things season 5 breakdown by VFX supervisor Betsy*
