Geoff's take: It's nothing new that tax incentives have a big influence on where film work gets done, but a trend of producers working with VFX earlier in the process is welcomed.

Incentives are shaping where post and VFX money lands, from New York to the UK. Vendors who join projects early and offer several disciplines under one roof look best placed. Worth watching how that shapes the packages you bid into.

## Incentives pull post capacity into place

Harbor's bigger New York base follows an extended state tax credit, which suggests incentives still steer where facilities invest. For a Copenhagen bidder, that may mean stronger US competition for multi-discipline post packages, and a reason to check which credits sit under any co-production.

- [Harbor Opens New Flagship Studios in New York, LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/harbor-opens-new-flagship-studios-in-new-york) · lbbonline.com *A large multi-discipline New York facility, tied by the source to the credit's extension to 2037, may sharpen competition for US-linked post work*
- [Harbor Opens Second New York City Post Studio in Hudson Square](https://postperspective.com/harbor-opens-second-new-york-city-post-studio-in-hudson-square/) · postperspective.com *Confirms the same opening and room count independently, and the credit link, so the expansion looks like a settled investment rather than a one-off claim*

## Early VFX involvement and indie co-production funding

The Loc Studios says UK credits are lifting independent features and that early input on recces and shoot planning gave its best results. That is a model worth copying in bids: sell supervision before the shoot. It is one UK studio's view, so treat it as a signal.

- [The Loc Studios expands into on-set VFX](https://www.televisual.com/news/the-loc-studios-expands-into-on-set-vfx/) · televisual.com *Shows a rival pitching early recce and planning input, and co-productions mixing UK, Canadian and Portuguese incentives, which may open doors for Nordic partners*

## Big shows and who delivered them

Credits and scale figures on tentpoles help benchmark crew size, hours and shot counts when pricing comparable work. They also show which vendors are winning visible sequences, useful when judging competitors and partners.

- ['Spider-Man: Brand New Day': Sony Pictures Imageworks' Web-Tastic VFX](https://www.awn.com/news/spider-man-brand-new-day-sony-pictures-imageworks-web-tastic-vfx) · Animation World Network *Gives usable benchmarks: 260 artists, over 101,000 hours and 449 shots, plus the simulation-heavy sequence the team called labour-intensive*
- [The Bluff: El Ranchito Showcases Storm and Crocodile VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/the-bluff-el-ranchito-showcases-storm-and-crocodile-vfx/) · artofvfx.com *El Ranchito's storm, ships and crocodile work is a reference for water and creature capability at a vendor you may meet in bids*

## Post planning at packaging stage

The piece argues producers should bring post in when packaging a project, to cut costs and ease casting. If it holds, it supports pushing for earlier VFX involvement in your own bids.

- [The rewards of putting post-production centre stage](https://www.c21media.net/perspective/the-rewards-of-putting-post-production-centre-stage/) · c21media.net *The teaser backs pitching post and VFX input at packaging stage, though without the full text the evidence is thin*
