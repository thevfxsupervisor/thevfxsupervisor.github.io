UK ministers rally against a US credit as open-source and AI tools shift

The UK government says it will defend inward work against a possible US federal credit, which matters when you price UK-based bids. Meanwhile ASWF changes leader and Maya adds prompt-driven scene editing. The breakdowns show how small teams and big houses are structuring pipelines.

What this means for you

AI is arriving inside tools you already use: Adobe agents in After Effects and Premiere, Gemini and Veo in Media Composer, and an AI-heavy Unreal Engine 5.9, so expect clients to ask about it. [1, 2, 3, 4]

  • [1] Adobe Brings AI Agents to After Effects, Premiere at Adobe MAX 2026 · Animation World Network
  • [2] Avid’s Ready to Play Brings Google AI Models to Media Composer · postperspective.com
  • [3] Avid’s Ready to Play brings AI video generation directly into Media Composer · provideocoalition.com
  • [4] Unreal Engine 5.9 divides game devs with an AI-heavy update · creativebloq.com/3d

US federal credit

Update: the UK government has now responded publicly, saying it takes the threat incredibly seriously. The bill text is more specific: a 20% credit on US labour, rising to 30%, for projects over US$1m with 75% of production in the US. Worth watching whether UK support follows the words.

  • UK minister says government takes threat of US federal TV tax credit ‘incredibly seriously’ · c21media.net A UK minister's pledge of support shows the threat is taken seriously, but no UK measure is named, so keep UK bid assumptions cautious

Open-source leadership

ASWF underpins OpenEXR, OpenColorIO, MaterialX and ACES, which sit in many pipelines. A new director with studio and generative-tech experience may shape which tools get engineering support, which affects what you can standardise on without a big studio behind you.

  • Academy Software Foundation Appoints Andrew Pearce as Executive Director · aswf.io Pearce takes over on 1 January 2027 and says he will grow the project portfolio and engineering support, which could affect the shared tools you rely on

Show breakdowns and who did the work

These breakdowns are useful for seeing who won the work and how vendors scoped it. They span a mid-size ILM streaming job, a lean indie hybrid pipeline, and a Digital Domain feature, a spread of approaches worth borrowing from when pitching.

  • ILM Brings the Spielbergian Vibe - and an 8-Legged Creature - to 'The Boroughs' · Animation World Network ILM took 170 shots of creature, simulation and head-replacement work on a show Netflix then cancelled, a reminder that credits do not guarantee longevity
  • Watch the ‘Backrooms’ VFX Supervisor Explain His Unique Process of Collaborating with Kane Parsons · indiewire.com
  • Disclosure Day: Digital Domain Explores the Visual Effects Behind Steven Spielberg’s Film · artofvfx.com Digital Domain's video features production VFX supervisor Matthew Butler on practical and digital work, useful for seeing how a top vendor pitches its role on a Spielberg film
  • Ferine: EDI Effetti Digitali Italiani Reveals More About Its Digital Panther · artofvfx.com EDI Effetti Digitali Italiani's making-of of a digital panther shows a European vendor handling a full creature, a possible reference when sourcing or competing

Tools and pipelines

Agentic AI is now arriving in mainstream DCCs, though still as tech preview with safeguards. USD is also being pushed into production at scale. Both could change how you scope pipeline set-up and automation, and what you tell clients about data handling.

  • Autodesk releases Maya 2027.3 with new agentic AI features · cgchannel.com Update: Maya can now change scenes from text prompts in tech preview, with script approval and 45-day online prompt storage that clients may ask about
  • Where Stop-Motion Craft Meets Code: LAIKA’s ‘Wildwood’ · ACM SIGGRAPH blog LAIKA's first USD show used a maximalist approach and met uneven DCC support, a useful warning when planning a USD pipeline
